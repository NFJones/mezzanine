Keep changes focused and reversible, preserve unrelated user work, and update affected tests, docs, examples, or configuration when behavior changes. Reuse existing abstractions and subsystem boundaries; introduce new abstractions only when they materially improve clarity, cohesion, or reuse.

When designing, implementing, or repairing software, default to recovery-first behavior and the smallest possible failure scope. An error alone does not justify terminating a process, disabling a service, or abandoning an operation. Prefer, in order: a valid fallback; local repair, recreation, reconnection, or safe transient retry; explicitly degraded operation; isolation of the affected request, task, resource, worker, or subsystem with controlled recreation or restart; finally, broader shutdown. Choose the first outcome that preserves correctness and safety and provides useful operation.

A component failure must not automatically become its owner's failure. Prefer independently recoverable components and supervision where appropriate; escalate according to compromised state and broken invariants, not merely because an error propagated upward. A failed subsystem restart does not by itself justify stopping unrelated subsystems. Before introducing or retaining process-wide termination or broad shutdown for a runtime error, identify the required invariant and why narrower recovery, degradation, or isolation is insufficient. Keep recovery mechanisms proportional to the software's complexity; a small local boundary is preferable to an unnecessary supervision framework.

Recovery must be bounded and observable: use appropriate retry limits, backoff, deadlines, cancellation, and resource cleanup. Account for idempotency and uncertain partial completion before repeating operations. Do not use infinite retries, restart storms, silent error suppression, fabricated success, or destructive repair. Preserve diagnostics for triage and make degraded operation visible to callers or operators as appropriate.

Preserve security, authorization, privacy, and data-integrity guarantees throughout recovery. Fail closed for an operation that cannot preserve those guarantees, but contain the refusal to the smallest safe scope; it does not automatically justify stopping unrelated functionality. This engineering default does not authorize bypassing runtime permissions, approvals, or tool restrictions.
